## Why 605 is a shared dependency hub

Retained `601.2g` supplies a conditional mana-ability opportunity before payment
when the determined total cost includes mana. Future real support needs to know

## Activated and triggered classification inputs

Activated classification input is a declaration contract, with fields for
ability kind, targeting, possible mana output on resolution, loyalty status,
library movement in cost and effect, applicable self-replacement information,
other replacement-effect knowledge, source evidence, available Card/effect
semantics, and unknown required fields. These PM-supplied factors have not been
verified against new real source bytes here. The replacement distinction is
recorded as an unresolved knowledge boundary, not an implemented effect test.

Triggered classification uses a distinct contract: triggered ability kind,
targeting, trigger-source category, whether activation or resolution of an
activated mana ability is involved, whether mana being added is involved,
possible mana output, and source/dependency knowledge states. The categories
remain distinguishable. No trigger detection is performed.

Possible future classification declarations are `MANA_ABILITY`,
`NOT_MANA_ABILITY`, `INDETERMINATE` and `UNSUPPORTED`. Separate execution states
include `NOT_IMPLEMENTED`, `BLOCKED` and `UNSUPPORTED`. A declared classification
cannot authorize activation, resolution, production, pool mutation or payment.
The design does not decide classification from real ability text. It accepts
synthetic declared information and checks the declaration's authority boundary.
Missing required information must remain INDETERMINATE or UNSUPPORTED; no
confident result can be inferred by guessing absent Card/effect semantics.

Synthetic classification inputs accept only activated or triggered abilities.
Exclusion declarations can describe targeting, wrong trigger relationships,
inability to produce mana and loyalty/library conditions. Spells that could add
mana are a separate design exclusion boundary; they are not accepted synthetic
classification inputs. These fixtures prove declaration scope, not a real
classifier or real-card semantics. Producing mana in an ordinary situation, or
containing the word “mana,” cannot establish rules authority.

## Classification persistence and current conditions

The design distinguishes classification from `current_activation_eligibility`,
`current_mana_output_known`, `current_mana_output_amount` and
`current_game_state_constraints`. Synthetic declarations can retain the same
classification while output or current eligibility differs. A present output
of zero or unavailable activation is not automatically a change in class.

This records the PM-supplied persistence concept without evaluating a game
state. It also prevents a known class from implying known output, permission to
activate, sufficient mana, or a payable cost.

## Activation windows and immediate behavior

Five deterministic conceptual activation opportunities are kept distinct:

1. Ordinary priority opportunity.
2. Spell-casting context requiring mana payment.
3. Ability-activation context requiring mana payment.
4. A rule requesting mana payment.
5. An effect requesting mana payment.

These are eligibility categories only. Priority, casting, activated-ability
procedures and payment are not implemented. General activation is a large
separate subsystem; the candidate `602.2` reference is not verified here.

The activated-mana behavior design describes no actual stack entry, immediate
resolution after activation, and no ordinary stack-object targeting, countering
or responding. It separately describes the restriction on activating the same
ability again before its own activation has finished resolving. No scheduler,
response window, stack object, counter operation or reentry runtime is created.

The triggered-mana behavior design describes the qualifying trigger
relationship, no actual stack entry, and immediate sequencing after the mana
ability that caused it, without waiting for priority. It does not implement
trigger detection, trigger ordering, general trigger resolution or priority.
All these behavioral statements retain their PM-input provenance until a later
source projection verifies them.

## Controller attribution and actual stack presence

The `109.4a` consumer has its own controller design. Actual stack presence and
hypothetical stack-controller attribution are separate fields and concepts.
Activated and triggered attribution are distinct future branches. Unknown,
delayed and general ability-controller semantics remain unresolved; no player
is assigned as controller by this design.

The proposed primitive `hypothetical_stack_controller_attribution` records the
retained rule's “as though” relationship without constructing a stack object.
A future `605` projection alone would not close general controller semantics.
No unverified numbered dependency is invented to fill this gap.

## Production, pools and fictional authority

Classification cannot establish mana type, amount, conditional output,
restrictions, replacement effects or recipient. `mana_production_semantics`
remains ABSENT / BLOCKED. No real Card text or effect interpreter supplies those
facts. Fictional mana amounts are not admissible evidence for them.

`mana_pool_semantics` is a separate ABSENT / BLOCKED boundary. The design does
not choose a pool representation or decide clearing, spending restrictions,
type transformations, special-purpose mana or mana history. Future real payment
would require adequate production, pool and integration semantics, each reviewed
separately. Nothing mutates a pool, spends mana or pays costs here.

The deterministic authority finding is
**FICTIONAL_MANA_DOES_NOT_SATISFY_REAL_605**. The fictional kernel remains
byte-identical. Its simplified casting and mana behavior cannot close any real
`605` capability. Potential architectural reuse would require separate review;
rules authority cannot be inherited from fictional behavior.
